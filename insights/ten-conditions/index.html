The Ten Conditions: why some banks put AI in production and most don't

Emil Matsakh · Founder, Catalyst-DNA · Former Chief Analytics Officer, top-10 global bank · July 2026

Nearly ninety percent of analytics investment at a top-10 global bank was consumed before reaching any value-creating activity. That number was not a technology finding. It was an organisational one — and it is the single most useful lens for understanding why most banks pilot AI and a small number put it in production.

The difference between those two groups is not budget, and it is not size. The leading institution in this space moves from idea to production in six weeks — a benchmark that is a function of design decisions, not scale. Every one of those decisions is available to a $2bn community bank on the day of its first project.

Ten conditions separate the institutions that deploy from the institutions that demonstrate. Five are organisational. Five are operational. The framework has one organising principle: weaknesses in the left column create ceilings on the right. No operational excellence survives an organisational gap above it.

The fastest AI deployers in financial services are not the least governed. They are the best pre-governed.

The ten conditions are not a maturity ladder to climb in sequence. They are a system — and like any system, it fails at its weakest point. A bank with world-class data infrastructure and no accountable business owner will produce pilots. A bank with committed leadership and project-built data will produce the same pilot three times.

Where to start. Three thresholds determine whether the next dollar of AI investment creates value or evaporates:

T1If no named P&L owner is accountable for your leading AI use case, stop investing in technology until one exists. Conditions 01, 02, and 05 come before any build.
T2If your last two analytics projects each rebuilt their own data from scratch, the next project must be a foundation, not a use case. Condition 07 is the compounding decision — a $10bn institution makes it on day one of its first project, or it does not make it at all.
T3If model approval takes longer than model development, governance is your constraint — and your accelerant. Pre-approved frameworks for standard use cases convert a six-month cycle into two weeks. Condition 09 before condition 08.
